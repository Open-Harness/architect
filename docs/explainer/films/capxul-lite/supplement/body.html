Capxul Lite, the whole design

A company's treasury on chain, controlled by its owners together. Budgets people and agents can spend alone. Books that record every payment with its reason. One contract, one server, and everything else made from the description.

The film says this in eight minutes. This page is the detail: every file, what falls out of it, where each thing has to go and why, and where the design strains. It is a design. Most of its syntax already exists in the repository or is decided; four ideas are new, and they are marked.

Exists in the repository, or is decided (K20, K23, K25, M6-chain, M6-money) Proposed by this design. Not built, not decided.

What you declare

Where things live

A store is a file. A chain store's contract takes its name from the file's path, and every thing that lives in it shares that one contract. The environment says which network.

The treasury and its owners

An owner is whoever holds a seat. That is a role declared as data, which is the only kind that can be rendered into Solidity. The last line is new: enough owners to reach the threshold, treated as one caller.

A budget

Its machine is the one examples/budgets already has, plus renewal. Renewing is a move made by time, in the syntax of K23. The rule that spent never passes the limit is said as data, so both languages can enforce it; today that check would sit inside a handler.

A spend, and the join

A spend is our note. It can't live on the chain because two of its fields are private, and your compiler already refuses a private field on a chain store. Its machine names every wait, and one state nobody plans for: money that moved with no note.

next is the join. One press of "Spend" is two operations, decided in two places, by one caller. Without next that sequence lives in a screen's code, where the model can't see it. With it, the button, the agent's tool, the journey's step and the trace all know the same thing.

What falls out

On the chain

The frame is generated and the behaviour is yours. The frame checks in the same order the server's wrapper does: the row, the role, the machine, your handler, the rules, the event. That is the rule of one enforcer per operation, with the contract as the enforcer. Each operation also gets a signed entry, so anyone can submit a call the holder signed.

    Where it strains

    1. Changing a rule on chain is a migration with money in it.The film changes renewal from a month to a week and the model says the contract is stale. That is true and it hides the hard part. A deployed contract can't be regenerated in place. Either it was built to be upgraded, or the money and the budgets move to a new one. The blueprint has no word for that yet, and it is the largest gap here.
    2. Owners together is one word that stands for a small system.Proposing, approving once each, counting, expiring, cancelling, and what happens if an owner is removed halfway. If the framework makes all that from one word, each of those choices needs a default you would agree with. The other way is to declare the proposal as a thing of your own.
    3. One contract holds every company's money.K25 has no contract per instance. So the treasury is rows in one contract, and its balance is a number in that contract, not an address of its own. Nobody can pay a company by sending to an address. If each company needs its own address, that is a factory, and the blueprint needs a way to say so.
    4. On a chain, "can" is a guess.Only the contract knows, and things change between asking and signing. The reference says what it expects. Trying the call is the real answer.
    5. A refusal on chain leaves no event.The server never hears "no" from the chain. The relayer knows when a submission is definitely refused, and that has to reach the spend's machine as its way out for "no".
    6. Anyone can pay around the app.The contract is a way in that you don't control. A holder can call it from any wallet and give a note id that matches nothing. So "unexplained" is a real state, and somebody has to be asked to explain.
    7. Time has no one to run it.Nothing wakes a contract on the first of the month. Renewal is worked out when someone next spends, or a keeper sends it. Either way the app must work out what is left by the same rule, from the same declaration.
    8. A person is not an account.Ada the person holds the budget only because one of her wallets is its holder, and an agent has an account of its own. The role on a spend, which is ours, has to cross from the person to the account.
    9. A handler in two languages is two handlers.The frame keeps them honest: the same checks, in the same order, with the same refusals. But the behaviour in Solidity and any behaviour in TypeScript are written separately, and only journeys show that they agree.

    What this is for

    The problem. An agent can write a system like this in an afternoon. What it can't give you is a reason to trust it the next morning, or a way to know what a change touches, without reading all of it.

    The position. You work on the system: the things, who may do what, what must always be true, and how the pieces join. You read that. The agent builds below it, the framework keeps every piece in step, and the running system is proved against stories you agreed to.

    Why a chain is the best test of it. It forces every hidden decision into the open: who has the last word, what is private, what money obeys, who has to say yes. Those are the decisions a person should be making, and here the blueprint makes you make them.
