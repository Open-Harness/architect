Do I have enough to do this?

Enough for most of it. One hole sits exactly where you care most.

The repo has a real command for nearly every step of running a system: describing, checking, proving, debugging with a trace, staging, promoting, watching, and working out what a change touches. Those can be filmed from real runs, the way the first film was.

The hole is "working with your agents". A new Orthograph app is given nothing for an agent: no instructions file, no skills, no roles. The repo plans one, the agent kit, and it is parked until after 1.0. What exists is this repository's own way of working, which is good and is proved every day, but it is our practice and not something an app developer is handed.

So the docs would have to write that part down for the first time. I think that is the right thing to do, and the same words can serve both readers: a manual page for you, and a skill file for your agent.

The docs can say plainly that these are not there. They should not pretend.

The shape: three parts, and one of them is the centre

Not the full Diátaxis split. You said it: the reader needs the primitives, how to read a blueprint, and how to manage the thing. So that is the whole table of contents.

Part one · the centre

The manual

How to build a real thing over time, with your agents. It follows the life of a system, from an idea to a change made months after launch. Each chapter says what you do, what your agent does, what you read, and what goes wrong. Each has a film.

Part two · short

Reading a blueprint

One guided read of a real one. What each kind of line promises, and what to look for when an agent hands you a change. First Plugged In, which is small. Then a slice of Capxul, which is not.

Part three · look things up

The primitives

One card for each, with many examples. What it is, the smallest example, the variants, what falls out of it, how it refuses, and whether it runs today. This is the canon, finished, with its commands and its API pages generated from the code.

What I would add to make this real

  • Write it once, in two forms. Each role is a manual page for you and a skill file for your agent, made from the same words. The page tells you what to expect. The skill tells the agent what to do.
  • Give a new app its instructions file. Today it gets none. This is the agent kit the repo parked, and the docs can't honestly teach "work with your agent" without at least a small one.
  • Be plain about what is ours. Ratchets, lanes and the board are how this repo builds Orthograph. The docs should teach them as a method, and say they are not yet commands.

How I would build it

A pilot first, then the workflow

  • Pilot. Three chapters with their films, to fix the format before anything is multiplied: journeys first, the builder climbs, and run it and read the trace. All three are real, and together they are the heart of the method.
  • Then fan out. You suggested a workflow, and this is the right shape for one. A writer for each chapter and each card, working at once. A second agent that checks every page against the code, the way the canon's check already does for quotes. One agent per film for the pictures, as I did today.
  • What stays with me. The outline, every narration script, and the last look at every film.

The rules I would hold it to

  • The canon stays the one place a word is explained. The manual links to it and never explains a primitive again.
  • Nothing is typed that can be read. Code is quoted from the repo and checked. Output is recorded from a real run. Commands and the API are generated.
  • Every page says how it stands: runs today, decided, practice, or missing.
  • No filler. A chapter has what you do, what your agent does, what you read, what goes wrong, and a film. Nothing else.

It is a large job: about twelve chapters, twenty cards and twelve films. Roughly thirty agents over several hours, and about ten minutes of rendering for each film.

  3. Do we write the agent kit now, as docs first?A new app gets no instructions for an agent today, and the repo has parked the kit until after 1.0. I think "working with your agents" needs at least a small one.
  4. Where do the docs live?I would finish the canon as the one source, add the manual beside it, and publish it as a page you can open now. A real site can come later.
